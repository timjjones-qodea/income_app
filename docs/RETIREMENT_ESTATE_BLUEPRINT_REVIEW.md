**Review date:** 15 September 2026  
**Planning start:** 6 April 2027  
**Status:** working design; figures require annual rate updates and professional sign-off

At 15 September 2026, Tim is 59 and Wendy is 54. Tim can access private pension benefits at the intended 6 April 2027 start. Wendy does not turn 55 until 23 May 2027, so—unless she has a protected pension age—her first crystallisation cannot occur on 6 April 2027 and should be scheduled on or after her 55th birthday. She reaches 55 before the normal minimum pension age rises to 57 on 6 April 2028.

## Decision summary

The proposed direction is sensible: use taxable pension withdrawals to fund retirement and regular gifts, preserve tax-free wrappers where useful, and keep evidence contemporaneously. It should not yet be executed as a fixed £180,000/£200,000 prescription.

The design must separate three questions:

1. What is the lowest-risk household retirement cash flow?
2. What gifts can be evidenced as normal expenditure out of each donor's income?
3. What pension withdrawals are worth making after allowing for Income Tax, investment risk, future pension IHT rules and each spouse's allowances?

## Corrections and qualifications to the source blueprint

### 1. The £180,000 withdrawal tax figure is an assumption, not a guarantee

The stated £67,203 Income Tax is arithmetically consistent with a £180,000 pension withdrawal using 2026/27 England, Wales and Northern Ireland main rates, with no Personal Allowance and no other taxable income. The resulting £112,797 net is therefore a useful baseline, not a guaranteed April 2027 outcome. The model must version tax rates by tax year and include other taxable income and PAYE timing.

### 2. Tim's supplied history indicates that the MPAA has not been triggered

Taking pension commencement lump sums does not by itself normally trigger the Money Purchase Annual Allowance, but taking taxable flexible benefits normally does. Tim has confirmed that he has taken tax-free cash only. A £20,000 gross contribution therefore appears to be within the standard £60,000 annual allowance, subject to confirming all current-year contributions, any pension input from other schemes, provider records and the precise contribution date.

The adjusted-net-income calculation must also use actual income and relief-at-source mechanics. A £20,000 gross personal contribution normally means £16,000 paid by Tim plus £4,000 basic-rate relief added by the provider, with further relief claimed or coded as appropriate.

### 3. Wendy's available PCLS is not automatically capped at £162,500

Twenty-five per cent of a £650,000 pot is £162,500, but the standard individual lump sum allowance is currently £268,275. Her actual entitlement depends on growth, prior benefits across all schemes, protections and scheme rules. Crystallising £200,000 in each of three years would use £150,000 of lump sum allowance; a fourth £50,000 crystallisation would produce another £12,500 if values and rules permit.

### 4. Wendy's PCLS should not be counted as income supporting exempt gifts

A pension commencement lump sum is capital in nature. It can fund spending or ISA subscriptions, but it should be kept out of the "income available for normal-expenditure gifts" calculation. This does not prevent the household from gifting; it means gifts must be traced to qualifying net income, with capital flows shown separately.

### 5. Regularity alone does not secure the IHT exemption

A standing order and letter of intent are useful evidence, but the exemption requires all three conditions: the gifts form part of normal expenditure, are made from income, and leave the donor able to maintain their normal standard of living from income. HMRC commonly looks for a pattern, although a properly evidenced first payment under a genuine commitment can qualify. Qualification is ultimately fact-dependent and may be tested after death.

Each gift must identify its donor. A household-level surplus is not enough: income, expenditure, account ownership and gifts need a defensible allocation between Tim and Wendy. Joint-account transfers need an explicit donor split.

### 6. ISA dividends are income, but changing the mandate does not create more wealth

ISA dividends are normally income in nature for this test even though they are tax-free. Paying them to cash improves tracing; it does not "artificially inflate" income. Natural yield varies, and preserving nominal ISA capital is not the same as preserving it in real terms. The model should include dividend cuts, inflation, fees and total-return scenarios.

### 7. ISA subscriptions are capital allocation, not household expenditure

Moving cash into an ISA does not consume wealth. It moves capital into a wrapper and should appear separately from lifestyle expenditure in the household model. Annual subscription limits and eligibility must be parameterised rather than assumed unchanged for twenty years.

### 8. Wendy's £10,000 tax buffer is a reasonable provisional reserve, not a tax calculation

Wendy has supplied 2025/26 profits of £34,000 from the UK property business and £6,500 from sole trade. Her £10,500 French-property receipt is after approximately £3,600 French tax, implying approximately £14,100 gross foreign profit for the starting UK calculation. Total gross profit is therefore approximately £54,600. Using 2025/26 England rates, no other income or adjustments, and the full Personal Allowance gives indicative UK Income Tax before foreign-tax credit of approximately £9,272. A full £3,600 foreign-tax credit would reduce this to approximately £5,672, but the allowable credit is capped and depends on treaty treatment and the filed figures. Her sole-trade profit is below the Class 4 National Insurance lower profits limit. A £10,000 cash reserve remains prudent, but the plan and app must retain gross foreign income, foreign tax, UK liability, credit allowed and payments on account separately.

The per-person split is deliberately provisional. The final normal-expenditure-out-of-income schedule should use actual income received, tax paid and expenditure funding. Boat expenditure should have its own category and payment trail. Legal ownership by Wendy does not by itself prove that all boat costs should be deducted only from her income, nor should costs be reallocated merely to maximise Tim's exempt gifting capacity.

Wendy's PCLS and any ISA subscriptions funded from it are capital movements and are not included above. State Pensions will be introduced in the years each spouse reaches the applicable State Pension age, using the then-current entitlement and uprating assumptions.

### 9. A flat 5% projection is not an adequate resilience test

A single immediate 20% fall followed by smooth 5% growth does not neutralise sequence risk. The plan should test variable returns, inflation, dividend reductions, longevity, care costs, tax-band changes and the death of either spouse. It should report depletion probability or at least a set of deterministic adverse paths, not describe the outcome as comfortable without a household balance sheet.

### 10. Pension IHT reform is now legislated, but implementation detail is still developing

Finance Act 2026 brings most unused pension funds and death benefits into estates for deaths from 6 April 2027. HMRC has said supporting regulations and guidance will continue to be issued. The app should therefore store rule versions and an annual review date rather than hard-code a permanent treatment.

## Proposed product boundary

### INCOME app: plan and forecast

- Household assets and liabilities, including ownership and estate treatment.
- Income streams with owner, gross/net basis, start/end date and tax category.
- Pension state: uncrystallised, crystallised drawdown, lump sum allowance used, and MPAA status.
- Versioned UK tax assumptions and transparent per-person calculations.
- Named scenarios for withdrawal, crystallisation, ISA subscription, expenditure and gifts.
- Annual sources-and-uses view separating income, capital movements, tax, spending and gifts.
- A gift-capacity result by donor: `max(0, net income - normal expenditure - safety margin)`.
- Stress paths and an assumptions/uncertainties register.

### MTD Bookkeeper: evidence and reporting

- Personal-bank transaction ingestion distinct from business MTD records.
- Actual income and normal-expenditure categorisation aligned to IHT403 section 2.
- Gift ledger recording donor, recipient, date, amount, account, method and purpose.
- Capital-versus-income provenance and joint-account donor allocation.
- Reconciliation of planned standing orders to bank transactions.
- Annual evidence pack: income, Income Tax, expenditure, gifts, surplus and exceptions.
- Attachments for pension statements, tax calculations, signed intentions and adviser reviews.
- Locked annual snapshots plus CSV/PDF export for executors and advisers.

The personal IHT ledger should be logically and visibly separate from Wendy's business and property MTD reporting, while reusing the existing import, rules, audit and reporting infrastructure.

## Delivery sequence

### Gate 1: establish facts before choosing withdrawal amounts

Collect the following for both spouses:

- dates of birth and UK tax jurisdiction;
- pension provider, scheme and policy identifiers;
- current crystallised/uncrystallised split and cost basis where shown;
- all historic PCLS/UFPLS/taxable pension events and protection certificates;
- MPAA status and flexible-access statements;
- current-year pension contributions from all sources;
- ISA/GIA/SIPP balances and natural income by owner;
- Wendy's rental and sole-trade taxable-profit bridge, not only gross receipts;
- State Pension forecasts and any DB pensions;
- household normal expenditure from at least 24 months of actual bank data;
- mortgages, other liabilities, life cover, wills, LPAs and pension nominations;
- intended recipients, amounts and whether gifts are equal or purpose-specific.

### Gate 2: build the auditable baseline

Implement the INCOME app's manual income streams, tax-year assumptions, pension state and annual sources-and-uses model. Import or enter two years of actual household expenditure into the MTD evidence ledger. Do not start a large fixed gift standing order until this baseline demonstrates sustainable surplus by donor.

### Gate 3: compare strategies

At minimum compare:

1. the proposed £180,000 Tim withdrawal;
2. withdrawals capped near selected tax thresholds;
3. dynamic withdrawals based on actual gift capacity;
4. alternative Wendy crystallisation schedules;
5. gifts from current income versus separate capital gifts/PETs;
6. survivor and first-death scenarios.

Rank them by after-tax household wealth, estate exposure, liquidity, gift capacity and resilience rather than IHT alone.

### Gate 4: provider and professional sign-off

Ask each provider to confirm partial crystallisation mechanics, payment frequency, PAYE handling, fees, minimum cash requirements, MPAA consequences and reporting. Have a Chartered Financial Planner and a STEP-qualified solicitor/tax adviser review the final scenario, wills, nominations and evidence design before execution.

### Gate 5: execute and review

Use separate recurring transfers with clear references, monthly reconciliation and an annual tax-year close. Recalculate safe gift capacity at least annually and after a material market, income, spending, health or tax-rule change.

## First implementation slice

The first code slice in INCOME should add versioned household assumptions and annual plan rows without attempting a fully automated recommendation. Acceptance criteria:

- every amount is owned by Tim, Wendy or jointly;
- every flow is classified as income, tax, normal expenditure, capital movement or gift;
- gross and net pension withdrawals reconcile through a visible tax calculation;
- PCLS is excluded from qualifying income;
- gift capacity is calculated separately by donor;
- all rates, thresholds and growth assumptions show their effective tax year and source/override status;
- scenario calculations are deterministic, tested and exportable.

The corresponding MTD slice should follow after its repository is explicitly placed in the implementation scope, because it is a separate deployed system and data store.

Under the current baseline assumptions—£2.727 million opening Tim SIPP, 5% annual growth applied before a £180,000 annual withdrawal—Tim's projected SIPP after 20 years is approximately **£1.284 million**, not £2.141 million. The earlier source blueprint's final-value figure is therefore not reproduced and should be treated as incorrect unless it used materially different timing or cash-flow assumptions. Wendy's modelled uncrystallised balance is exhausted during 2030/31 after growth and phased crystallisation. Modelled PCLS totals approximately £182,014, leaving about £86,261 of the standard lump sum allowance unused because entitlement is also constrained to 25% of funds crystallised. Her total SIPP then continues growing because no taxable drawdown is assumed.

The baseline produces approximately **£2.807 million** cumulative provisional gift capacity over 20 years before introducing safety margins. This is a nominal planning total, not an exempt-gift recommendation: later tax rules, actual expenditure, fluctuating income and the evidence tests can materially reduce it.

Known limits added by this slice:

- deterministic smooth growth is not a substitute for variable-return or Monte Carlo stress testing;
- SIPP growth uses one shared rate and does not yet model fees or different asset allocations;
- ISA values and Wendy's non-pension profits are held flat in nominal terms while ISA income is paid out;
- annual Tim withdrawals are flat in cash terms and occur after modelled annual growth;
- the State Pension dates reflect the current timetable and remain editable because policy can change;
- Wendy's May 2027 access is represented within the 2027/28 tax year rather than as monthly cash-flow timing;
- future Income Tax bands and allowances remain frozen at the chosen known tax-year rules and are visibly identified as such.

The next analytical slice should compare saved scenarios side by side and introduce adverse return paths, fees, income changes and a survivor/first-death case. The MTD Bookkeeper evidence ledger should then consume actual rather than planned income, tax, expenditure and gift transactions.
